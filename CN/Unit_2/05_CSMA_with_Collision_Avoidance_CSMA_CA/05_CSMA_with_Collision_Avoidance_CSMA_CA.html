1. Why CSMA/CD Fails in Wireless Networks

In wired Ethernet, collision detection is performed by sensing voltage spikes ($V_{channel} > 2 \cdot V_{trans}$). In wireless media, radio signal power attenuates rapidly with distance ($1/r^2$). Due to transceiver dynamic range limitations, a station's powerful transmitter blinds its sensitive receiver amplifier, making real-time collision detection virtually impossible.

2. Hidden and Exposed Station Dilemma

  • Hidden Station Problem: Stations A and C cannot detect each other's RF signals due to physical distance or obstacles. Both sense the channel as idle and transmit to intermediate Access Point B simultaneously, resulting in catastrophic collision at station B.
  • Exposed Station Problem: Station C unnecessarily refrains from transmitting to D because it senses active transmission between A and B, despite D being far outside B's interference range.

3. 4-Way RTS/CTS Handshake & NAV Virtual Sensing

NAV Duration Equation

Duration_RTS = SIFS + T_CTS + SIFS + T_DATA + SIFS + T_ACK
NAV_CTS = SIFS + T_DATA + SIFS + T_ACK

Every station overhearing a CTS sets its Network Allocation Vector (NAV) countdown timer to the duration value, silencing transmissions until the complete data-ACK cycle finishes.
